The Evening

An evening of music and historical reflection

Free At Last? places the African American Spiritual at the center of the evening. Through music and spoken word, its chapters trace a journey through endurance, sorrow, resistance, and the celebration of freedom.

Born among African Americans held in slavery, the spiritual carried faith, sorrow, resistance, and hope across generations. An opening chant gathers the voices before the spirituals that follow. Through solo songs, duets, and shared performances, the program brings individual expression into conversation with communal memory, and Dr. Johnnetta Betsch Cole’s narration connects each chapter, inviting listeners to consider the lives and histories carried within the music.

?

The question in the title remains open. Alongside expressions of deliverance, the program asks what freedom requires of those who inherit its unfinished promise.

Hear beloved spirituals including “Deep River,” “There Is a Balm in Gilead,” “Ride On, King Jesus,” and “He’s Got the Whole World in His Hands,” performed by sopranos Alison Buchanan and Ann Marie McPhail, lyric baritone Robert Sims, and pianist Artina McCain.

The Narrator

Dr. Johnnetta Betsch Cole

Narrator

Dr. Johnnetta Betsch Cole is an anthropologist, educator, and cultural leader whose work has strengthened institutions devoted to Black history, art, and opportunity.

Born in Jacksonville, she entered Fisk University at fifteen, graduated from Oberlin College, and earned master’s and doctoral degrees in anthropology at Northwestern University. Her academic career has included teaching and leadership appointments at Washington State University, the University of Massachusetts Amherst, Hunter College, and Emory University. Her books include Conversations: Straight Talk with America’s Sister President and Gender Talk.

Her connection to Northeast Florida includes a family history of advocacy for Black life and culture. Her sister, MaVynee Betsch, was an opera singer who became the environmental advocate known as the Beach Lady of American Beach.

As narrator of Free At Last?, Dr. Cole brings her scholarship and her commitment to cultural understanding to a program that invites audiences to hear these songs as expressions of faith, endurance, and the continuing pursuit of freedom. On November 20th, she shares it with her own parish, St. Peter’s.

The Artists

Four artists, one living tradition

Alison Buchanan

Soprano

British soprano Alison Buchanan brings an international career in opera, concert performance, and artistic leadership. As artistic director of Pegasus Opera Company, she champions composers and performers of African and Asian heritage and creates opportunities for emerging singers.

Read full biographyShow less

A graduate of London’s Guildhall School of Music and Drama and Philadelphia’s Curtis Institute of Music, Buchanan completed the Adler Fellowship at San Francisco Opera, where she made her debut as Mimì in La bohème. She has performed with orchestras including the London Symphony, Baltimore Symphony, Sydney Symphony, Boston Symphony, and Philadelphia Orchestra. Her collaborators have included Sir Colin Davis, Sir Simon Rattle, André Previn, and Marin Alsop.

Her recordings include Peter Grimes with the London Symphony Orchestra under Sir Colin Davis and songs by George Walker with the composer at the piano. In 2021, she curated Mami Wata at the Royal Opera House, celebrating vocal music by Black and Middle Eastern women. Her screen appearances include Queen Charlotte: A Bridgerton Story. Across her work as a singer and artistic director, Buchanan maintains a commitment to expanding opportunities in classical music.

Ann Marie McPhail

Soprano

Atlanta native Ann Marie McPhail is a lyric soprano whose work spans opera, concert music, film, and education. She made her professional debut as the Countess in Mozart’s The Marriage of Figaro with Houston Ebony Opera and performed at the closing ceremonies of the 1996 Centennial Olympic Games.

Read full biographyShow less

Her screen credits include Tyler Perry’s For Colored Girls. On the operatic stage, she has appeared as Strawberry Woman in Gershwin’s Porgy and Bess with Atlanta Opera. In 2019, she was soprano soloist in the premiere of Bill Banfield’s SHINE at the National Association of Negro Musicians centennial convention in Chicago. She also sang in Atlanta Ballet’s 2020 premiere of Sunrise Divine.

McPhail has performed with companies including San Francisco Opera, Opera Ebony, and Houston Ebony Opera, and with the Savannah Philharmonic. Her teaching appointments have included Clark Atlanta University, Spelman College, Agnes Scott College, Talladega College, and Grambling State University. She holds degrees in music education from Clark Atlanta University and voice performance from Georgia State University, and brings a particular commitment to interpreting the African American spiritual.

Robert Sims

Lyric Baritone

Lyric baritone Robert Sims has devoted much of his career to interpreting African American spirituals and folk songs. A Gold Medal winner of the American Traditions Competition, he made his Carnegie Hall solo debut in 2005 with a special appearance by Odetta. He returned in 2009 at Jessye Norman’s invitation for Honor! A Celebration of the African American Cultural Legacy.

Read full biographyShow less

Sims has performed the leading baritone role in The James Baldwin Chronicles with Renee Baker and the Chicago Modern Orchestra Project, and Pangloss in Bernstein’s Candide with the Oakland Symphony. His work with Chicago Opera Theater includes the world premiere of Dan Shore’s Freedom Ride.

He has toured Germany, Austria, and Switzerland with Roland Carter and the inSpirit Male Chorus, performing spirituals by Noah Ryder, Carter, and Roland Hayes. He also produced Masters of the Spiritual at Lincoln Center.

Sims founded Three Generations, a touring group devoted to American spirituals and folk songs whose artists have included George Shirley, Kenneth Overton, William Warfield, and Benjamin Matthews. He has also performed with Simon Estes and Jubilant Sykes as Simon, Sykes & Sims.

Artina McCain

Piano

Artina McCain combines an international performing career with teaching, recording, and advocacy for music by underrepresented composers. Her recital appearances include Wigmore Hall, the Barbican Centre, Walt Disney Concert Hall, and Weill Recital Hall at Carnegie Hall. She has also performed with the Dallas and Memphis symphony orchestras.

Read full biographyShow less

Her recordings include I, Too, a Naxos release featuring African American spirituals and art songs, and collaborations with her husband and duo partner, trombonist Martin McCain. She arranged Twenty-Four Traditional African American Folk Songs for intermediate pianists, published by Hal Leonard.

Drawing on her recovery from a performance injury, McCain advocates for musicians’ wellness through articles, lectures, and educational programs. Her story has been featured on the BBC podcast Sideways.

McCain earned degrees from Southern Methodist University, the Cleveland Institute of Music, and the University of Texas at Austin. She is associate professor of piano and coordinator of the keyboard area at the University of Memphis, and co-founder and director of the Memphis International Piano Festival and Competition.

Founded in Jacksonville in 2002 by clarinetist Terrance L. Patterson, Ritz Chamber Players presents world-class chamber music that centers the artistry of composers and performers of African descent. Now in its Silver Jubilee Season, RCP connects audiences with the richness of the classical tradition through performances, new commissions, education, and community engagement.
